History

The railway in Rudy appeared on March 25, 1899. At that time, a narrow-gauge line
(track width 785 mm) leading from Gliwice through Nieborowice to Rudy
with a length of 23.5 km was put into operation. This was the first stage of the project providing for the extension
of the railway line all the way to Racibórz. On February 28, 1901, the railway track reached
the village of Paproć. However, on April 1 of the following year, a further section from Paproć to Markowice Raciborskie was put into operation.
The last fragment of the line
built in stages was extended to Plania (now Płonia) – a district of Racibórz on May 17, 1903.
The entire route from Gliwice to Racibórz through Bojków, Nieborowice,
Pilchowice, Stanice, Rudy, Szymocice, Nędza and Babice totaled 51 km. Already
in the first year of existence, the railway transported 280,000 passengers and 10,000 tons
of goods. It should be emphasized that this was the most convenient means of passenger transport
on the Gliwice-Racibórz route. Travel by standard-gauge passenger train,
with the necessary change in Kędzierzyn took about 2 hours and 20
minutes. The narrow-gauge railway, although it had comparable travel time, served
10 localities that until then had no efficient communication. Until 1945,
transport reached 700,000 people per year, while in 1949-1963
it did not fall below 1 million. A record was recorded in 1954 when 1,834,400 passengers were transported.
In 1991 – the last year of regular operation
under the PKP banner, over 90,000 people used this railway's services.
