Cistercian in literature

This is how August Potthast, a historian and doctor of philosophy, described the "Cistercian" in 1858
in his work "History of the Ancient Cistercian Monastery in Rudy in Upper Silesia",
published on the occasion of the six hundredth anniversary of the founding of the abbey: "A thoughtful
wanderer quietly strolls along the winding gravel paths of the park and gazes thoughtfully at the old,
tall trees near the palace, among which one attracts particular
attention; I mean the giant oak, which like a monument to the history of Rudy rises behind
the beautiful church and whose leaves whisper a tale about the beginnings of the monastery's existence
in the times when the tree was planted..." Even earlier, in 1752, the dignified
dimensions of the "Cistercian" were noticed by J. E. Ridinger, placing the contours of the oak on
an engraving made using the mezzotint technique, the subject of which is the abbey, and in
particular the image of Our Lady of Rudy.
