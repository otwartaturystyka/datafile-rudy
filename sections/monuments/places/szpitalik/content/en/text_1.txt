Juliusz Roger

Juliusz Roger (1819-1865) is undoubtedly one of the bright figures
associated with Silesia. He was a German, born in Niedelstozen near Ulm, and besides
his work as a physician and naturalist, he became known as a passionate lover of folk culture
and a social activist. He arrived in Silesia in 1847, when he took up
the position of court physician to the Duke of Racibórz, Wiktor I. Soon after
he received the title of Royal Sanitary Councilor. Dr. Roger, besides caring for
the health of the ducal couple, engaged in sacrificial help for the poorest,
organizing a kind of public healthcare system in these areas. In 1858
he founded St. Charles Hospital in Rudy, the maintenance of which was taken over by the ducal
couple, Wiktor and Amelia. Treatment was provided here for small fees or even
free of charge. Dr. Roger also initiated the construction of a new hospital in Rybnik,
ultimately built several years after his death, as a result of the efforts of the Duke
of Racibórz. The distinguished physician and social activist was also a collector of folk songs.
Having learned Polish, he collected 546 local songs, which
(along with the compilation) he published in 1863 under the title "Songs of the Polish People in
Upper Silesia". He also maintained contacts with Polish writers - Józef Lompa and
Paweł Stalmach. What is particularly noble and worthy of respect: Dr. Roger as
a native German openly admitted that Silesians are Poles, which he expressed
in letters to his German friends.
