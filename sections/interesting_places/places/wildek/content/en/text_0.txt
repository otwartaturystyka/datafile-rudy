Legend

In the 19th century, between Kuźnia Raciborska, Nędza, Szymocice and Jankowice Rudzkie
stretched the "Thiergarten", a game park belonging to the Dukes
of Racibórz. This was a forest area of almost 2,500 hectares,
intended exclusively for ducal hunting. To protect the forest,
the owners built stylish forester's lodges in which they settled guards —
foresters. According to this legend, one such guard named Eliasz
lived in the Wildek forester's lodge. He was famous for his cruelty. With his permission, only
local people or workers could enter the forest. Everyone had to whistle loudly
or sing. The forester then knew if it was one of his own. If someone without his
permission entered the forest, they were immediately killed or captured and thrown into
a dungeon in the basement of the forester's lodge, where starvation awaited them. One time a
seller of holy pictures appeared in the forest. The forester shot at him, but
he caught the bullet and said: "Now I will direct it at you". Eliasz
got scared and promised to stop killing. However, the story for Eliasz probably
didn't end well, because he soon died, and then a flock of 100 ravens flew over the forester's lodge
and circled for a long time. Local residents
said it was a terrifying sight.
