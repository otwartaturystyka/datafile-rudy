Third Silesian Uprising

On November 11, 1918, Poland regained independence, but the fate of Silesia
was to be decided by a plebiscite, which took place on March 20, 1920. The plebiscite was won by
Germany, including with the votes of 200,000 emigrants living outside the borders
of Silesia, but the majority of the population on the right bank of the Oder sided with Poland.
In connection with the above, Wojciech Korfanty proposed establishing
a border on the Oder. Since this division was not accepted by the Entente
states, on the night of May 2/3, 1921, the Third Silesian Uprising broke out. The uprising
began with the "Bridges" action, during which bridges on the Oder from Koźle
to beyond Opole were blown up, which prevented the Germans from sending reinforcements to their units in
Upper Silesia. At the same time, the "South" Operational Group blocked Racibórz and
the entire Oder line to Bierawa. During the uprising, this group effectively
restrained demonstrative actions of enemy groups from beyond the Oder,
sometimes fighting fierce battles. On the morning of May 9, insurgent units began
an attack on Kędzierzyn. At night, the railway junction was captured, and the next day
the port in Koźle. After the initial successes of the insurgents, there was some stabilization
of operations. However, on May 2, the Germans, attacking with overwhelming forces, occupied St. Anne's Mountain
and broke deep into the insurgent military positions. On June 4, the Germans
began an offensive in the St. Anne's Mountain area, including capturing Kędzierzyn and the
Koźle port. On June 11, the fighting forces were separated by a demarcation line, but
complete cessation of hostilities occurred only on June 25, after
the armistice was signed by the German side.
